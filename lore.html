Religion

The Old Gods

Lower Planes · Chatoic · Sealed

The Old Gods were those of evil. Another name for the Old Gods would be the Lower Gods as they now occupy The Lower Plane. These gods used to rule over Ai' Taleth, but were banished to the The Lower Planes by The New Gods 2800 years ago during The Great Betrayal when Felnor, The Betrayer, betrayed the Old Gods and helped The New Gods send them throughout The Lower Planes.

The New Gods

Upper Planes · Orderly · Most accepted

The New Gods are the deities of The Higher Planes and have the largest religious following in Ai' Taleth. They represent good and order as they influence The Material Plane from above.

The Lord of Light

Monotheism · Anti-Magic · Extreme · Young

The Lord of Light is the monotheistic god followed by the Gaimond Kingdom. Those who follow him are known for their extreme anti-magic beliefs and imperialistic views. By his followers, he is believed to be the being that saved the world from the Devastation.

Major Historical Events

The Devestation (800 years ago)

Arcane ritual went wrong. Asmodeus, The King of Hell and Sapila, the Goddess of Rot, were both successfully summoned to the Material Plane, resulting in the destruction of the Holy Valir Empire and the creation of the Infernis and the Fungril.

PLACEHOLDER The Long Silence (300 years ago)

Three centuries during which the gods did not answer prayers. Ended abruptly, and nobody agrees on why.

PLACEHOLDER The Founding of Highspire (180 years ago)

Refugees from the broken kingdoms gathered around the white cliffs and raised the walls of what would become the capital of Veshmar.

Ancestries

The peoples of Ai'Taleth, sorted into three categories. Full ancestries reference →

Common

  • Clank — Humanoid machines of metal, wood, stone, or other materials, built to serve a purpose.
  • Dwarf — Mountain-dwellers of Thul; many cities are carved into peaks, though dwarves also live in larger mixed cities.
  • Elf — Long-lived recluses of remote Thul, split among Wood, High, and Sand branches; the oldest still remember the Devastation.
  • Giantborne — Descendants of giants who came to Thul; some live in mountain tribes, others as farmers and laborers in human cities.
  • Halfling — Whimsical, magical people of the Great Plains; once warred with goblins until aided by the Holy Valir Empire.
  • Human — Originally of Valiris; Dorar Marcus brought them to Thul about 1,500 years ago.

Exotic

  • Dracona — Dragon-descended people of the Gellmir Mountains (red, black, white) and Khard (green, blue).
  • Faun — Feywild origin, now in the Weirwood; forest guardians who trade, dance, and guard the woods.
  • Firbolg — Bovine, fey-touched humanoids who shepherd nature and its ties to the Feywild.
  • Galapa (no notes yet)
  • Katari — Reclusive explorers of the natural world who travel throughout Thul.
  • Ribbet — Energetic frog-folk of humid bogs; common in northern Thul and on Raften.
  • Simiah — Native to Raften; some claim Zerg origin and greater technology.

Shunned

  • Faerie — Rare, mysterious insect-like humanoids often seen as a bad omen and avoided.
  • Fungril — Born in 0 A.D. with Sapila, Goddess of Rot; descended from the Blessed Isles (Fungal Isles).
  • Goblin — Inventive raiders of the plains; mostly driven into Barbaria after the Thousand Year War.
  • Infernis — Born in 0 A.D., descended from fiends that overran Valiris after the Holy Valir Empire fell.
  • Orc — Warlike people of Barbaria's plains; some have migrated to the Gaimond Kingdom, Free Cities, or Faelán.
